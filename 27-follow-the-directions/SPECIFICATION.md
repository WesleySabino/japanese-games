## Original catalogue content (English translation)

### 27. Follow the Directions

1. **Name:** Follow the Directions
2. **Concept:** the player moves a character on a grid map by following written Japanese instructions — reading is acting.
3. **How it works:** a 7×7 grid with emoji landmarks (🏦 ぎんこう, 🏫 がっこう, 🏪 コンビニ, 🌳 こうえん). A 1–4-step instruction appears; the player taps cells/arrows to execute it. Reaching the correct destination earns a star; the route is evaluated step by step (the exact error is recorded: confused みぎ/ひだり? ignored 二つ?).
4. **Action per round:** read the instruction → build a mental model → execute.
5. **Skill:** understanding instructions (〜て, 〜てください, 〜たら), directions, numbers, and positions (まえ, うしろ, となり, かど).
6. **Why it helps:** inspired by *Total Physical Response*, but through reading: comprehension is verified through action, without translation. Linking steps with 〜て trains reading of long, segmented sentences.
7. **Level:** upper beginner → pre-intermediate.
8. **Example:** 「まっすぐ 二つ すすんで、みぎに まがって ください。ぎんこうの まえで とまって ください。」 (*Go straight two blocks, turn right, and stop in front of the bank.*) Level 6: 「コンビニの かどを 左に 曲がったら、公園の となりの 店に 入って ください。」
9. **Progression:** 1 step → 4; conditionals (〜たら); landmark-relative instructions; kanji (右, 左, 前, 後ろ); larger map; limited reading time (the instruction disappears before acting: this requires retaining meaning rather than rereading).
10. **Data reuse:** instruction generator over `mapTemplates[]` + direction vocabulary; all sentences generated by a template grammar.
11. **Complexity:** low.
12. **Cost:** very low.
13. **Replayability:** medium-high (procedural maps).
14. **Risks:** narrow lexical field — use it as a “phase-specific” game and integrate it into Word Dungeon (33) to avoid saturation.
