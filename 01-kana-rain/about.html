A LITTLE RAIN. A LITTLE READING.

Read the rain. Keep it clear.

Kana Rain is a free browser arcade game for hiragana and katakana. Read hiragana by typing its sound, then match katakana with hiragana choices. Track correct answers, mistakes and expired drops before time or hearts run out. Begin with Hiragana and choose your difficulty, then enter the garden.

How to play

  1. Make two choices. Start with Hiragana and choose Easy, Normal or Hard on the main menu. Katakana becomes available after completing Hiragana; existing gardens keep their earlier access. Every regular run has three hearts and a three-minute timer.
  2. Get ready. A three-second countdown gives you a moment to prepare. You can skip it.
  3. Read and match. For Hiragana, type romaji such as あ → a or し → shi, or use the sound pad. Correct typed answers submit automatically; short answers such as n wait briefly, or Enter submits immediately. Any visible matching hiragana can be answered. For Katakana, select a falling drop and choose its partner from three hiragana buttons, such as カ → か. Click an option or press 1–3, following the displayed numbers. Its options stay in the same places for that round. No romaji or Japanese keyboard is needed for Katakana.
  4. Keep the rain clear. On every difficulty, rain speeds up as you answer correctly and eases off after each drop that reaches the pond. A drop reaching the pond costs a heart and clears unfinished input. Losing all three hearts ends the run. Wrong answers show the reading, stain the drop and make it fall faster.
  5. Follow your practice. During play, follow correct answers, time and large central hearts. Wrong answers and expired drops appear in the final summary. Corrections add a correct answer; each wrong submission adds a mistake; expiration does not add another wrong answer. Keep building your streak. An ! warns when a drop is close to the pond. Pause with Escape or the Pause button; menus and leaving the tab pause automatically.

Difficulty controls new kana

Rain speed, hearts and run length stay the same. Difficulty changes how cautiously new kana return after misreadings.

Any wrong submission or expired drop pauses new kana until ten consecutive first-try hits on familiar drops. Two expirations or four familiar first-answer misses in your selected recent window also close a longer brake. That brake needs the full window, the chosen hit target, at most one expiration and fewer than four misses to reopen. These are recovery targets after a pause, not requirements before every debut. Assisted first-try hits count; correcting a wrong answer does not. Recent history continues between runs. Katakana uses its own history for the longer brake, so earlier Hiragana mistakes cannot hold it back; the ten-hit pause remains shared.

Grow your kana garden

Begin with the five hiragana vowels: あ い う え お. New hiragana initially show their sound; new katakana show their hiragana partner. Each available kana needs two correct first answers and at least 85% submitted first-answer accuracy to open the next row. Hints and pronunciation do not block unlocks. For new gardens, Katakana opens after all 27 hiragana groups are available and all 104 hiragana have two correct first answers and at least 85% first-answer accuracy. Earlier Katakana access and progress remain available.

New nonstarter kana wait for three consecutive first-try familiar hits before and between introductions. Hiragana introduces at most two nonstarter kana per run. Katakana allows two newly introduced nonstarter kana still needing practice at a time; reaching two correct first answers and 85% first-answer accuracy frees a slot for another. Only one debut appears on screen. Any wrong answer or expiration holds new kana until ten consecutive first-try familiar hits. Two expirations or four familiar first-answer misses in the recent difficulty window also close a longer brake. Starter vowels respect these brakes when familiar content exists; one vowel can bootstrap an empty alphabet. Earned rows remain available.

My Kana Garden shows familiarity, next-row readiness and pronunciation tiles. Optional Learning details retains independent and assisted evidence. Katakana choices practise recognition and remain assisted evidence, rather than independent recall. These estimates do not certify mastery or long-term retention.

Results focus on correct answers, wrong answers and expired drops. First-try hits, corrections and best streak remain as secondary details. Tap mistake cards to listen, or choose Review these kana for a focused one-minute round with three hearts.

Hear it. Read it. Recall it.

Kana pronunciations use bundled, synthesized Japanese recordings. Listen through lesson tiles, the target Listen button and results review cards. Sounds and small celebrations mark clean hits, streaks, unlocks and heart loss.

The interface and instructions are in English. The sound button mutes pronunciation and effects. Reduced effects in Settings calms decorative motion without changing the challenge.

For Hiragana on phones, the sound pad opens for play: tap the sound family, then the sound, so し is S/SH → shi and ん is N → n. The keyboard button switches to your device keyboard; a physical keyboard also works. Katakana uses three hiragana buttons on phones and computers, without opening a keyboard.

Short runs, saved progress

The game saves progress in this browser when storage is available. If saving fails, a persistent warning offers a progress export while play continues. Export a backup from My Kana Garden. Your earned rows and learning history are preserved; historical point records remain unchanged in the export. New practice uses answer counts and does not overwrite or convert those records.

After the first online visit prepares the offline garden, supported browsers can load the cached game and kana recordings without an internet connection. Wait for Your garden is ready offline before relying on offline play.

Frequently asked questions

Can I start without knowing hiragana?

Yes. The five vowels introduce the sounds with reading hints and pronunciation tiles. Hints disappear as you recognize them. Easy gives new kana more recovery time after misreadings; all levels have three hearts and the same adaptive rain.

When can I play Katakana?

For new gardens, Katakana opens after all 27 hiragana groups are available and all 104 hiragana have two correct first answers and at least 85% first-answer accuracy. Earlier Katakana access and progress remain available. My Kana Garden shows exactly which sounds need more first-try answers before the next row opens.

Do I need an account or an installation?

No account or installation is required. Open Kana Rain in a modern browser with JavaScript enabled. Keyboard and touch controls are built in.

Does progress follow me to another device?

Progress is saved in the browser used to play. Another browser or device has its own progress, and clearing site data can remove history. Export from My Kana Garden for a backup.

Can I play offline?

The initial visit needs an internet connection so the game and audio can be cached. Once the menu confirms that the garden is ready offline, supported browsers can play the cached game and recordings offline.

Can I pause without losing my run?

Yes. Pause freezes the timer, drops and acceleration. Partially typed answers remain when you resume. An actual drop expiration clears unfinished typing, so a stale answer does not carry into the next drop.

Is this a complete Japanese course?

Kana Rain is an arcade game about recognizing hiragana and katakana readings. Use it for playful reading practice alongside wider Japanese study.
